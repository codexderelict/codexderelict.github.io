About me

Introduction

My name's Omar. I'm a 22 year old from the Emirates, with an interest in language, mathematics, technology and where they all combine! I speak Arabic, English (native), German (B2) and French (C1).

How I got into computational linguistics


I got into linguistics when I was about 7 years old from looking at random Web 1.0 linguistics sites (some of which should be linked in the Links section of the site). I began self-studying German on my own when I was 14, and studied French formally at 17 at Sorbonne University Abu Dhabi (where I also studied German from A1 because it turns out I had quite a few holes in my understanding).

Around that time is when I got into mathematics. I had believed I was unable to think mathematically (despite being good at it as a child, it was just arithmetic). I began self-studying mathematics with an interest in seeing how things work and developing intuition for mathematical rules and processes. It hit me, eventually, that the same kind of pattern recognition and rules for legal construction are those I've already internalized when learning a language. Curiously, I decided to explore the relations between language and math, stumbling upon formal language theory, formal semantics and, of course, NLP.

My research interests:

  • Formal/probabilistic models of morphosyntactic change, especially in Semitic languages
  • The relationship between morphosyntactic function and form using models informed by linguistic formalisms and information theory
  • Neural language models as linguistic objects of study by studying embedding representations and their potential correspondence to facets of formal models
  • Furthering mathematical models of natural language uniting continuous vector representations and statistical/corpus methods with discrete linguistic formalisms such as LFG, HPSG, dependency and categorial grammar
